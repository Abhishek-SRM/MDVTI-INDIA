What you would run

A teaching centre, not a studio roster — one structured path from practice to therapy to teaching.

Three rooms, one centre.

A Yogkulam centre is a small institution: a hall for practice, a room for therapy and a desk that keeps the records straight. Each part of it is supported by something already in place at the institute.

The practice hall

PGDYT · Yoga teacher training

Batches for the Post Graduate Diploma in Yoga Therapy and the Diploma in Yoga Teacher Training run here, in Hindi, with notes in English. Students work through philosophy, anatomy, asana, pranayama, meditation and teaching method across the full programme.

What makes a hall work is less the room than the batch: students who stay for a full programme, taught by faculty who know the syllabus.

Supplied to this room

  • Curriculum and detailed study material
  • Virtual classroom access
  • Recorded lectures and live doubt sessions
  • Faculty recruitment and training

The therapy room

Specialist modules · consultations

The therapy room is where specialist modules are taught — face yoga, prenatal yoga and the therapy modules — and where a centre can hold consultations with people who come for a specific condition rather than a general class.

It is the room that most clearly separates a Yogkulam centre from a neighbourhood studio, and the one that asks the most of your faculty.

Supplied to this room

  • Specialist module material
  • Guidance to establish and operate the centre
  • Staff recruitment and training
  • A dedicated support manager

The front desk

Admissions · accounts · records

Every enquiry, admission, fee and certificate passes through this desk. It runs on the franchisee login panel, which keeps student records, post tracking and accounts in one place rather than in a notebook.

Students' complaints and questions reach a system, not a personal phone: that is what lets a centre grow past the person who started it.

Supplied to this room

  • Franchisee login panel
  • Accounts management and post tracking
  • Complaint management and CRM
  • Admission forms and the franchise kit

From an enquiry to a first batch.

Five steps. The second one is a conversation, not a sales call — it is where you find out whether your city and your space suit a centre at all.

Open the application form
01

Tell us the city

Send the enquiry form or call the franchise team. The useful details are simple: where you are, what space you have, and whether you have taught before.

02

Talk it through

A call about the district, the space, the programmes a centre can run and the terms — including the fee, which we put in writing rather than quote on a poster.

05

Open the first batch

Admissions open with marketing support behind them. Faculty training, the virtual classroom and live doubt sessions run with the batch from its first week.

Ask us these four things before you sign.

Which programmes can this centre run?

The institute teaches the PGDYT, the Diploma in Yoga Teacher Training and specialist modules. Which of them your centre runs is agreed with you, and recognition is stated programme by programme — YCB, IYA, Yoga Alliance USA and ISO 9001:2015 apply where they are named, not to every course by default.

What exactly does the fee cover?

Ask for the fee and its inclusions written out: the kit, the login panel, the training, the marketing support and the length of the agreement. Ask for it in writing before anything is signed. A number agreed on a phone call is not a term.

Is my district mine?

Territory is a written term, not a verbal one. Ask what has been agreed for your district and whether another centre already operates there before you commit to a premises.

What will it earn?

We will not quote you an income, and you should be wary of anyone who does. A centre earns from the students it admits and teaches; what that comes to depends on the district, the faculty and the work put in.

Questions partners ask us.

If yours is not here, the franchise team will answer it on the phone — including the ones this page cannot answer with a number.

Do I need to be a yoga teacher already?

Not necessarily. What matters is a serious practice, an understanding of what a teaching centre does, and the ability to run a place and its people. Faculty can be recruited and trained with the institute’s help.

Do I need my own premises?

Yes. The application asks for the premises, its address and photographs, along with a document and owner details. The space needs room for a practice hall and a consulting room; guidance on setting it up follows the agreement.

In what language are classes taught?

Classes are taught in Hindi, with notes and study material in English. That combination is deliberate: students should be able to teach where they live, and read beyond the classroom.

What support do I get in the first months?

The kit and affiliation letter, the franchisee login panel, faculty recruitment and training, marketing material, business and social media training, and a named support manager. The full list is in the section above.

How long does it take to open?

It depends on the premises and the documents, and on how quickly the terms are agreed. We will not quote a fixed number of days; the realistic answer comes out of the first conversation.

Can I speak to an existing centre?

Ask us. Where there is a partner near you who is willing to talk, we will try to put you in touch before you decide.

Is the franchise fee published?

No single number is published, because the terms depend on the district and the programmes a centre will run. Ask for the fee and what it includes in writing.

I am already a partner. Where do I log in?

Use the franchisee login from the top bar or the footer. If your centre’s access needs resetting, call the franchise team on 1800-891-9232.
